# The Plata Clan: disguise greetings, the one line a disguised Plata says when the conversation window opens.
# On first read each must pass for a friendly villager on the road. On a re-read, something is a little wrong:
# too interested in where you're headed, stone, silver, "little ones", counting, walls, the dark under things,
# knowing too much about you. Never say "silverfish" or "Plata" here. Never assume rain, a clear sky or the hour
# (the line can show at any time). 1-3 sentences.
# Pools by biome group: sand (desert, badlands), stone (peaks, windswept hills, other stone country),
# green (plains, forest, meadow, roads), cold (snow, ice, taiga). The "any" pool mixes into every group.
# Placeholders: {player}, {name} (the disguise name). Use sparingly.
# Format: [pool.name] starts a pool; one entry per line; # starts a comment. Compile with build_lines.py.

[disguise.greet.sand]
Hot enough to bake bread on the rocks, isn't it? You'd think nothing could live out here. You'd be surprised what keeps cool under the sand.
Every grain of this sand was a stone once, you know. Somebody broke it. Somebody's always breaking something.
Water? Kind of you, but no. I don't need much. Never have.
Mind the dunes, friend. Folk come out here digging for the old temples, and the ground remembers who dug.
Good day! Where are you bound? East? West? Down?
You've sand in your boots and stone dust on your sleeves. Busy hands, for a walker.
The badlands keep their colours in stripes, one stripe for every year. I like a land that keeps count.
I'd offer you shade, but the only shade out here is underneath things. It's very pleasant, underneath things.
Watch out for husks, and for holes. Mostly the holes. You never know who's at the bottom of one.
Funny, meeting someone in all this empty. I'm told I'm hard to spot. Grey on gold, you'd think it would be easy.
You walk heavy, traveller. Out here the sand carries every footstep a long way down.
Sandstone, they call it. As if the stone's gone soft and sleepy in the sun. It hasn't. It's only waiting.
Lost in the dunes? No shame in it. The dunes wander about. The stone underneath never does, and neither do some of us.
Ho there! Come for the cactus fruit, or the buried rooms? Everyone comes for the buried rooms, sooner or later.

[disguise.greet.stone]
Steep climb, isn't it? Catch your breath. The mountain isn't going anywhere, and neither is anything inside it.
Careful on this stretch. Some of the stone up here is rotten. It breaks far too easily, and what's inside isn't always rock.
Lovely view, this. You can see every hole anyone's ever dug in that hillside. I like to know where they all are.
That's a fine pick on your belt. Well worn, too. I'd love to hear where it's been.
The goats won't come near this bit of the pass. Clever animals, goats. They can hear the stone breathing.
Up from the caves, were you? They run for miles under here. Ever wonder who's down in them while you're up top?
Sit, if you like. Not on that rock. Or that one. They're spoken for.
Mind your step. Every stone up here is somebody's home. My mother always said so, and she'd know.
You came up the long way, over the top. Most folk just tunnel straight through these days, whatever's in the way.
Grey rock, grey sky, grey old me. I blend right in, don't I? Ha! Sit a while.
People get lost up here all the time. The mountain never loses anyone, mind. It knows exactly where they all are.
Hello, friend. Don't mind the grit on my coat. The wind blows it about. Mostly the wind.
These hills are hollow, you know. Full of little rooms, full of little lives. Best walk softly.

[disguise.greet.green]
A friendly face on a quiet road! You'd be surprised how many folk walk straight past me and never notice I'm here.
Good road for walking, this. Some folk cut right through the hills instead. Rude to the hills, I always think.
Hello! I've been watching the road a good long while. You're the first one along who looked interesting.
Fine old woods. Old stumps, old stones. I like to turn the stones over and see who's living underneath.
Mind the path, it's full of beetles. Little things. I never step on little things. Do you?
Grand old trees, these. Their roots go right down into the stone and hold on. Some things hold on whatever you do to them.
You came from the village back there, didn't you? The one with the stone well. Lovely well. Deep. I know it from the bottom up.
Where's the road taking you, friend? Somewhere with a cellar, I hope. I'm very fond of cellars.
Pretty meadow, isn't it? Not a stone in it, if you don't look closely. There's always a stone, if you look closely.
A walker's legs and a miner's hands. That's an unusual pair, friend. Which do you use more?
Have you come far? Over the hills, or under them?
Stop a while, stop a while! The flowers don't mind. The bees don't mind. Nothing out here minds me at all.
Bless you for stopping. Most travellers on this road are in such a hurry to get underground.

[disguise.greet.cold]
Brr! Come stand by me, out of the wind. I don't feel the cold much myself. I grew up somewhere colder. And darker.
Snow hides everything, doesn't it? Footprints, holes, old stones, people. I find it very restful.
Out in this, on foot? You must be on an errand. Or digging for something. Which is it?
I'd offer you a fire, but I've nothing to burn, and I don't much care for the light anyway.
Don't mind the frost on me, I've been standing here a while. Waiting for the weather to turn. Or for somebody.
The spruce folk say the ground freezes solid in winter. It doesn't, you know. Not all the way down. Things keep moving down there.
Lost in all this white? Stay close. I know every stone under this snow. I've counted them.
Cold hands? Tuck them in your sleeves. I keep mine in mine. I keep a lot of things in my sleeves.
A long way from any hearth, aren't we? The two of us, out here, with nobody to see.
Listen. The snow's so quiet you can hear the whole world under it, if you lie still. Scratching away.
Fine furs. And a fine pick, too, chipped right down to the haft. A busy winter, was it?
Ho there! I was starting to talk to the stones. They don't answer. Not so anyone up here could hear, anyway.
Careful on the ice, friend. It cracks long before it breaks. Most things do, if you've the ears for it.
