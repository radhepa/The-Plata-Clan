# them). {mother} is the Mother you killed most recently ("Mother Ysolde"). Lines show in the window, so they may run
# longer than speech lines (220 characters at most). Placeholders: {player}, {name}, {rank}, and in truce.hideout
# {where} (a direction, "off to the north-east") and {far} ("not far", "a fair walk", "a long walk").

# truce.greet: when the window opens. truce.mother: "Who was she?" truce.why: "Why let me be?"
# truce.clan: "Tell me about the clan." truce.hideout / truce.nohideout: "Where does the clan sleep?"
# truce.bye: the player says farewell. truce.enough: they've answered four questions and go.

[truce.greet]
Well? You've got my attention. Use it before I think better of it.
Talking, is it? All right. I've never spoken to a Mother-killer before.
Go on, then. Ask. I might even answer.
Strange. A season ago I'd have had your throat. Now we're chatting.
Say your piece. The stone can wait a moment.
I'm listening. Keep your hands where I can see them.
Ask me anything. Except where I sleep.
Make it quick. The others won't like me talking to you.

[truce.mother]
{mother} was the oldest thing in that Warren. Older than the stair. She molted more times than you've had birthdays.
She raised half of us out of the husk. Fed us, named us, taught us to curl up small and listen to the rock.
{mother} never left the Hollow. Not once in thirty winters. She didn't need to. The walls brought her everything.
She could hear a pickaxe three valleys over. She'd tell us whose it was, and how many little ones it had killed.
Some say she was one of the miners who sat down in the stronghold dust. Others say she was the first silverfish's daughter. I believe both.
{mother} sang to the brood every night. You'd never know it from the Keen. It's the same voice.
Every Mother takes a new name at her last molt. {mother} was hers. I never learned the old one.
She was kind to us. Not to anyone else. But to us.
She made the coffer. Every scale in it is one of ours, shed and kept. She could tell you whose, for each one.
{mother} used to say the stone is patient, and so are we. I suppose you were more patient.

[truce.why]
The clan has a law older than the Warrens: whoever fells a Mother walks free for a season. Not forgiven. Free.
Because you'd win. I saw what you did to {mother}. I'm not stupid enough to want that.
The others will fight you. Let them. Someone has to be left to remember her.
I'm tired. Maybe that's the truth. Shedding skins, sleeping in walls, counting the dead. I'm tired.
{mother} wouldn't want her children thrown at you one by one. She was cleverer than that.
Because you talked to me first. Nobody talks to us. They just swing.
I wanted to see what kind of person kills a Mother. You look disappointingly ordinary.
Don't mistake it for kindness. I'm letting you be. That's all.

[truce.clan]
We are in the stone. That's the whole of it. Every wall, every road, every cellar you've ever slept over.
There are more of us than you'd think. Your baker. Your ferryman. That nice old shepherd. Maybe.
We count. Every silverfish you crush is a mark against your name. Some of you have very long names.
Scuttlers run the roads. Shardcasters keep the camps. The Wardens keep the young ones in line. And the Mothers keep everyone.
A Warren is a hill with a Mother under it. A den is a few of us and a fire. I like dens. Less climbing.
We sleep standing up in our old shells. It's warm, and nobody bothers you. Mostly.
The mark? A silverfish seen from above. Feelers, segments, three tails. You'll find it everywhere we've ever lived.
We were villagers once. Some of us still are, by day.
We don't hate you, stone-breaker. We just remember you. From the outside it looks the same.
Every Warren has its Mother. Every Mother has her coffer. Every coffer has the mark. That's the clan.

{where}, {far}, if you must know. Don't wake the little ones too rudely.

{where}. And if anyone asks, you found it by yourself.

[truce.nohideout]
Nowhere near. We've moved on from these parts. Can't think why.
None close. The stone's empty round here.
Nowhere you'll find. I'm not that grateful.
Far from here. Farther than you'd want to walk.

[truce.bye]
Into the stone, then. Don't look for me.
Go well, stone-breaker. Watch the walls.
That's enough for one day. Enough for a lifetime, maybe.
I'll see you on the road. You won't see me.
Farewell, {player}. Tell nobody I was kind.
Off you go. And no more stairs, all right?

[truce.enough]
That's more than I've said to anyone in years. I'm done.
Enough questions. The walls are calling me home.
You've had your answers. I've had my curiosity. We're even.
